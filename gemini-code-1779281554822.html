SFM: Food Security Application

Derivatives $f'(x)$ in Malaysian Agriculture

Optimizing Oil Palm (*Elaeis guineensis*) Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) production yield rates using calculus to ensure national agricultural sustainability.

The Food Security Problem

In Malaysia, oil palm is a critical pillar of agricultural GDP and global food security (vegetable oil supply). Oil palm trees have a specific biological lifespan where Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) yield peaks and eventually declines. For plantation managers and food security analysts, understanding the exact rate of yield change at any given tree age is vital for planning replanting cycles and forecasting national food stockpiles.

Data Reference & Realism Note: The parameters used in this model are adapted from realistic physiological growth curves published by the Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) (e.g., "MPOB Oil Palm Yield Profiles by Region and Age"), where commercial yields typically peak between years 9 to 15 before experiencing a gradual biological deceleration.

The Production Function: $f(x)$

Yield $y$ (Tonnes per Hectare per Year, $t/ha/yr$) modeled against tree age $x$ (Years) using a localized polynomial growth curve:

$$f(x) = -0.012x^3 + 0.38x^2 - 1.8x + 5$$

Valid for commercial lifespans: $x \in [3, 25]$ years.

The Rate of Change: $f'(x)$

Applying the Power Rule of differentiation yields the marginal growth rate formula (the derivative):

$$f'(x) = -0.036x^2 + 0.76x - 1.8$$

Measures instantaneous yield acceleration/deceleration in $(t/ha/yr)$ per year.
